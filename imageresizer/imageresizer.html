Image resizer

Resize, crop, compress or change the format of an image for the website, the newsletter or social media.

How it works: you choose an image and what should happen to it. You can crop it to an exact format, only change its size, keep its size and change only the file type, or fit it in an exact format without cutting anything off.

A JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, AVIF, SVG or HEIC (iPhone) image.

What should happen to the image?

The image keeps its original size. Choose the new file type in step 3.

Save it

Questions

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens inside your browser, on your computer.

What is the difference between the four options?

Crop gives an image of exactly the size you ask for, by cutting off what does not fit (you choose what). Only change the size keeps the whole image and its proportions, so the result can be smaller than the size you typed. Exact format, nothing cut off gives the exact size and keeps the whole image by adding a background around it. Keep the original size does not touch the size at all: only the file type (and the quality) change.

Which file type should I choose?

JPG for photos, PNG for logos or images with text and transparency, WebP if the place where you use it accepts it. For JPG and WebP, a quality around 80 to 85% is usually a good balance between sharpness and weight.

I changed a PNG with a transparent background into a JPG: why is it white now?

JPG cannot be transparent, so the transparent parts are filled with white. Keep PNG or choose WebP if you need to keep the transparency.

The preview says the image is too small

When the original is smaller than the format, it has to be enlarged and may look blurry. Use a larger original, or a smaller format.

What happens with an animated GIF?

Only the first image of the GIF is used, and the result is a normal still image (JPG, PNG or WebP). The animation is lost.

My AVIF image is not accepted

AVIF is read by your browser, so it only works in recent versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. If it does not open, try another browser.

What happens with an SVG drawing?

The drawing is turned into a normal image, large enough to stay sharp (at least 1600 px on its long side), and you can then resize it like any other image. Choose PNG to keep a transparent background. The SVG file must have a width and a height.

Can I add my own formats to the list?

Yes: they are listed at the top of imageresizer.js and can be edited there, or you can just type any width and height.

Can I use a photo from an iPhone (HEIC)?

Yes. The photo is first converted inside your browser, which can take a few seconds for a large photo, and then you can resize it like any other image. If the conversion fails, export the photo as JPG on your iPhone or Mac and use that file.

Everything runs in your browser: your image never leaves your computer.
