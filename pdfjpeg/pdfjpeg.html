PDF to JPG

Turn the pages of a PDF into JPG images.

How it works: you choose a PDF, say which pages you want and how sharp the images should be. One page gives one JPG; several pages give a zip file with one JPG per page. Your PDF is never changed.

Questions

Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens inside your browser, on your computer.

What do I get?

For one page, a single JPG. For several pages, a zip file with one JPG per page, named after the PDF and the page number, for example report_p01.jpg, report_p02.jpg. Open the zip first, then use the images.

Which sharpness should I choose?

“Standard” is enough for the website, a newsletter or a slide. Choose “High” only if the image will be printed or if small text must stay readable when you zoom in. The higher it is, the heavier the files.

The background of my page is white, but it was transparent

A JPG cannot be transparent, so the page is always drawn on a white background.

The page says the PDF is protected

The file needs a password. Open it, remove the protection (or print it to a new PDF), and try again.

The text or the fonts look different from the PDF

If the PDF does not contain its own fonts, your browser uses similar ones. Open the PDF in a PDF reader, export it again with the fonts included, and try again.

The page says it was made a little smaller

A very large page (a poster, for example) at high sharpness would be too big for the browser, so it is reduced to fit. Choose a lower sharpness to avoid it.

Everything runs in your browser: your PDF never leaves your computer.
